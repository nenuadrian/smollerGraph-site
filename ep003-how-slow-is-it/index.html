    Graph database benchmark: Neo4j vs Kùzu

    A first benchmark on a million relationships, with the same two-hop question asked of smollerGraph, Neo4j and Kùzu.

    21 October 2026 · Watch on YouTube

    We measure smollerGraph against Neo4j and Kùzu on a made-up social graph of a hundred thousand people and a million relationships, generated with a fixed seed so each machine gets an identical one. sg load reports how long loading takes, a new sg bench command times a two-hop walk from a thousand people, and a Python script loads the graph into Kùzu and Neo4j, in Docker, to ask them for those people too. We chart the results and go through why smollerGraph is slow where it's slow, and quick where it's quick, for now.

    Why, for now

    18:11 So why is smollerGraph slow at one and quick at the other? When it loads, it reads a line, adds a relationship, and reads or writes six pages through a system call each, one relationship at a time, on one core. That's about six million system calls for a million relationships. Kùzu and Neo4j load the graph in bulk instead. They read the CSV files on several cores, sort what they read, and write their files in large sequential pieces, not a page at a time. When it walks, smollerGraph is a function call inside our own program, written for one question, walking two hops of one type. Kùzu and Neo4j are answering a Cypher query, which they parse, plan and run, through their Python libraries, and in Neo4j's case over Bolt, to a server in a container. So the walk isn't a fair fight yet, as smollerGraph has no query language to pay for, and no transactions to keep. And all three were measured warm, with their files in the operating system's cache, which is what makes our page reads cheap. These numbers are where we start from, and the plan is to measure again as the buffer pool, the index and the Cypher subset come in.
